Problem (unicode2)
(a): UTF-8 完全兼容 Unicode，根据不同的 Unicode code point 灵活选择 1/2/3/4 bytes 来表示，UTF-16 对大部分字符都用 2 bytes，UTF-32 都使用 4 bytes；因此对于相同的给定文本， UTF-8 通常情况下的编码字节数更短
(b): 这里说明 UTF-8 的编码规则：

Unicode 范围              UTF-8 二进制格式

U+0000   ~ U+007F         0xxxxxxx

U+0080   ~ U+07FF         110xxxxx 10xxxxxx

U+0800   ~ U+FFFF         1110xxxx 10xxxxxx 10xxxxxx

U+10000  ~ U+10FFFF       11110xxx 10xxxxxx 10xxxxxx 10xxxxxx

因此不能对单个字节直接调用 decode()，否则有可能会报错，例如以 10 开头的字节没有起始字节。
报错示例：UnicodeDecodeError: 'utf-8' codec can't decode byte 0xe4 in position 0: unexpected end of data
(c): 逻辑同 b，可以用 11111111 11111111，第二个字节不以 10 开头

The proportion of FLOPs consumed by attention increases significantly from 8.75% to 60.54%, while the proportion consumed by SwiGLU decreases from 54.96% to 23.77%. This is because attention has quadratic complexity \(O(L^2)\) with respect to sequence length, whereas the other components scale linearly with \(L\). Consequently, attention becomes the dominant computational bottleneck for long-context inference.

Problem(learning_rate)
We swept peak learning rates logarithmically, using seed 336, batch size 32,
40,000 steps, 2,000 warmup steps, cosine decay to 10% of peak LR, and a fixed
validation sample of 8 batches of 16 sequences (32,768 tokens). The final
per-token validation losses were: 1e-4: 1.5982; 1e-3: 1.3778; 3e-3: 1.3843;
1e-2: 1.4857; 3e-2: 1.8658; 1e-1: 2.1318. These runs completed without
numerical divergence; among them 1e-3 produced the lowest measured final loss.
The original 3e-4 baseline was evaluated separately on 131,072 tokens and
obtained 1.430875; its training-time validation used a different fixed batch.
Each sweep's full curve and conditions are in runs/lr_sweep_<LR>_seed336/;
comparison plots are in runs/learning_rate_comparison/. Additional higher-LR
runs investigated the requested divergence boundary.
Peak LR 0.3 exceeded the predeclared loss threshold of 100 at step 1,107
(training loss 105.7918) and was stopped as divergent; its curve through
step 1,000 is retained in runs/lr_sweep_0.3_seed336/. Thus the divergence
threshold in this sweep lies above 0.1 and no higher than 0.3 in peak LR;
0.001 gave the best final loss among the completed finite runs, far below
that threshold. The changing learning rate and coarse search limit this
conclusion: this is not a precise estimate of a constant-LR stability edge.
The best observed checkpoint (peak LR 0.001) was independently evaluated on
131,072 validation tokens using seed 336, giving loss 1.364318, below 1.45.
Peak LR 1.0 also exceeded the loss-100 divergence threshold at step 523
(loss 121.5833); its logged curve is runs/lr_sweep_1_seed336/metrics.csv.

Problem(batch_size_experiment)
For an initial equal-token comparison, we trained from seed 336 using peak
LR 0.001, 5% warmup and cosine decay to 0.0001, and approximately 10.24M
tokens per run. Fixed validation uses 32,768 tokens. Batch size, steps,
final validation loss, and wall-clock seconds respectively were:
1: 40000, 2.0214, 896.76s; 8: 5000, 1.9742, 152.06s;
32: 1250, 2.0708, 121.43s; 64: 625, 2.2267, 117.29s;
128: 313, 2.5189, 112.25s (10,256,384 tokens).
At this short, fixed-token budget, larger batches used the GPU more efficiently
but performed fewer optimizer updates and had higher final validation loss;
batch 8 achieved the lowest loss of these settings. The same peak LR was used
for all settings, so this is not a fully LR-retuned comparison. Learning-curve CSVs are
under runs/batch_<size>_seed336/metrics.csv and include wall-clock times.
Additional equal-token runs: batch 256, 157 steps, validation loss 2.9619,
112.82s; batch 512, 79 steps, loss 3.4811, 110.98s. Batch 1024 ran out of
memory on the first forward pass on the A800 80GB. Thus 512 worked and 1024
did not; the exact maximum between them was not determined. The larger runs
processed 10,289,152 and 10,354,688 tokens respectively due to rounding.
Additional attempts at batch 768 and 640 each ran out of GPU memory during
the first backward pass. Thus 512 is the largest tested working batch and
the exact maximum is between 512 and 640, subject to allocator and device state.
Comparison plots against token count and wall time are in
runs/batch_size_comparison/validation_vs_tokens.png and validation_vs_time.png.
